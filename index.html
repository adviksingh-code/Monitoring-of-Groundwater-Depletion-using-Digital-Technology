01 — THE PROBLEM

Groundwater recharges slowly. Punjab is drawing it down fast.

Groundwater meets nearly nine-tenths of India's rural drinking water needs and over half its irrigation needs. Unlike a river, it fills back up slowly — so once an aquifer is over-drawn, recovery can take decades rather than years.

Punjab, home to Lovely Professional University, is among the most groundwater-stressed regions in the world. Decades of paddy-wheat cropping on subsidised tube-well power have pushed water tables down sharply, and the Central Ground Water Board now classifies most blocks here as "over-exploited."

Old monitoring methods check water levels only a few times a year — too slow to warn anyone before a well runs dry. That gap, between data and the people who need it, is what this project tries to close.

The tools to measure this already exist.

Government and scientific infrastructure already tracks groundwater at a national level — the gap is in getting that data to the person holding the well bucket.

NASA / GRACE

Satellite gravimetry confirms rapid loss

A widely cited study using NASA's GRACE satellite mission showed groundwater in north-western India — Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan — depleting fast, mainly from unsustainable irrigation rather than lower rainfall.

CGWB

Most Punjab blocks are "Over-Exploited"

The Central Ground Water Board classifies blocks as Safe, Semi-Critical, Critical or Over-Exploited based on extraction-to-recharge ratio. Most of central and southern Punjab now sits in the worst category.

India-WRIS / DWLR

Digital infrastructure already exists

India-WRIS integrates data from thousands of monitoring stations. CGWB's growing network of Digital Water Level Recorders automatically transmits readings, replacing old manual quarterly checks.

THE GAP

Dashboards are built for hydrologists, not farmers

This data rarely reaches the individual farmer in a form they can act on. That's the specific gap BhuJal Nigrani is designed to close.

BhuJal Nigrani — "Groundwater Watch"

STEP 1

Sense

A low-cost sensor inside the borewell — similar in principle to CGWB's DWLR units — checks the water level every few hours.

STEP 2

Predict

Readings travel over GSM or LoRaWAN to the cloud, where they're combined with India-WRIS and CGWB data and forecast 1–3 months ahead.

STEP 3

Act

A multilingual app shows a simple green/yellow/red status with SMS fallback and crop advice — no internet required to receive an alert.

04 — WHY IT MATTERS

Better information changes decisions.

ENVIRONMENTAL

Act before the point of no return

Early alerts support timely action — micro-irrigation, a crop change — before an aquifer tips into "Over-Exploited," supporting UN SDG 6, Clean Water and Sanitation.

ECONOMIC

Fewer failed crops, fewer deep wells

Advance warning and crop guidance help farmers avoid failed harvests and the rising cost of drilling ever-deeper borewells.

SOCIAL

Science, in the language of the person who needs it

Local-language SMS and a simple app bring groundwater data to smallholder farmers directly — not just to officials — while aggregated data helps panchayats target recharge structures under schemes like Atal Bhujal Yojana.
